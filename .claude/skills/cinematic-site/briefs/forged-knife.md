# Forged chef's knife — "the blade remembers the fire"

## Identity
- **slug** `forged-knife`
- **Niche** Hand-forged kitchen tools
- **Physics** Material state change — molten → plastic → quenched → honed
- **Brand names** `OKUMI` · `EMBERLINE`
- **Palette** `--soot #0A0A0B` `--ember #D8551F` `--forge-gold #E8A33D` `--steel #6E7B85` `--ash #CFC8BE`
- **Type** Condensed industrial sans headings + generous serif body. Mono for specs.

## Scene beats
1. **Opening** — Black frame. A single orange glow, out of focus. Camera pulls back slowly: a bar of steel at white heat resting in the forge, the coals breathing.
2. **Hammer** — Rhythmic strikes. The bar flattens and elongates under each blow, sparks arcing toward the lens and dying. Camera tracks along the lengthening steel.
3. **BLUR-THROUGH — the quench** — The blade plunges into oil. A violent steam and vapour bloom swallows the entire frame. *This is the best natural transition in the whole brief library — protect it.*
4. **Emergence** — The vapour thins. A dark, oxidised, unfinished blade, cooling, matte.
5. **Honing** — Camera travels *along the edge* at extreme macro as the whetstone passes. A mirror bevel and damascus water-pattern emerge in its wake. Tack sharp.
6. **Resolution** — Pull back fast. The knife is mid-air over a tomato on end-grain wood; it passes through and the halves fall apart.

## Ending frame
The two tomato halves separating, blade still in motion, shallow depth of field. Copy sits upper-left on the dark ground.

## Products
Gyuto 210mm · santoku 165mm · petty 120mm · 3-knife roll · whetstone. Premium (£180–£620).

## Difficulty
**Medium–Hard.** Fourth build. Three genuine material states in one continuous move.

## Gotchas
- The hammer is the failure point — models render the strike as a static blur. Prompt "hammer head fully visible at the top of its arc, then contact, then rebound".
- Keep hands out except as a backlit silhouette; no fingers near the edge.
- Damascus pattern needs "flowing watery layered pattern etched into the steel surface" or you get generic brushed metal.
- The final cut must be its own extension clip — generating it inline reliably produces a floating knife that never contacts the tomato.
