# Aged spirit — "cold, then fire"

## Identity
- **slug** `spirits`
- **Niche** Single-cask whisky / aged spirit
- **Physics** Refraction, internal ice structure, fracture, pour
- **Brand names** `CASK & CREVASSE` · `ANTEDILUVIAN`
- **Palette** `--glacier #9FC3D4` `--amber #B5701C` `--oak #4A3524` `--night #0C0B0A` `--brass #C9A45C`
- **Type** Engraved-style serif, letterpress feel. Small caps. Tight and confident.

## Scene beats
1. **Opening** — A perfect clear ice sphere in total darkness. Condensation beading on its surface. One cold rim light. Slow rotation.
2. **Into the ice** — Camera pushes in until the ice fills the frame: internal crevasses like a glacier, air bubbles frozen mid-rise, refracted light splitting.
3. **BLUR-THROUGH — the flood** — Amber liquid pours in from above and floods the crevasse structure. Cold blue becomes warm gold as the frame fills with moving liquid and light.
4. **Refraction** — Camera pulls back out through the glass wall. The room behind bends and inverts through the tumbler. A fracture line races across the ice with an audible crack.
5. **Garnish** — The glass sits on dark oak. An orange twist drops in and releases a fine oil mist that catches the light beam.
6. **Resolution** — The glass lifted toward camera; the room falls out of focus behind it.

## Ending frame
Tumbler raised, amber lit from behind, oak and darkness beyond. Copy sits low-left in the shadow.

## Products
The single cask (70cl), a 12-year, a 3×20cl tasting set. £65–£320. **Age-gate interstitial required** — ask the user about target market before building it.

## Difficulty
**Medium.** Ice and refraction are almost impossible to make look cheap.

## Gotchas
- Refraction through glass is the one thing to prompt explicitly: "the background visibly bends and inverts through the glass and liquid".
- The fracture beat is fast and models skip it. Give it its own extension clip if the first take ignores it.
- No label text. Generate a blank or wax-sealed bottle; wordmark in HTML.
- Do not let the liquid render as flat orange — "deep amber with visible viscosity and legs on the glass".
